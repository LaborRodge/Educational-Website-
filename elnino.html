EL NIÑO

Story

El Niño is a climate pattern that happens when parts of the Pacific Ocean become warmer than usual. This change affects weather conditions in different parts of the world, including the Philippines. During El Niño, the country may experience hotter temperatures, less rainfall, and longer periods of dry weather. When there is less rain than normal, rivers, dams, and other water sources may become low. Some communities may experience water shortages, making it difficult for families to get enough water for drinking, cooking, cleaning, and farming. Crops may also dry out because they do not receive enough water, which can affect farmers' income and the supply of food. El Niño can also increase the risk of drought, forest fires, and extreme heat. People, especially children, older adults, and outdoor workers, may experience heat-related illnesses if they stay under the sun for too long. It can also affect animals and plants that depend on regular rainfall. The effects of El Niño remind us how important water conservation is. Communities need to use water wisely, protect natural resources, and prepare for possible droughts to reduce its effects on daily life.

When rainfall is lower than normal, rivers and water sources may become low. Crops can also dry out.

Consequences

  • Water shortage
  • Damaged crops and lower harvests
  • Very hot weather and heat-related illness
  • Greater risk of grass and forest fires

How Should We Prepare?

  • Save water and avoid wasting it.
  • Drink enough water.
  • Stay in shaded or cool places during very hot hours.
  • Listen to PAGASA and local government announcements.
